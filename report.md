# Report

## Structure
1. Problem and Motivation — Why the problem matters; what you are trying to discover.
2. Dataset and Data Objects — Source, scale, unit of analysis, attributes, measurement types, limitations.
3. Data Quality — Missingness, duplicates, outliers, errors, and decisions made.
4. Preprocessing and Feature Engineering — All transformations and why they were appropriate.
5. Exploratory Data Analysis — Key descriptive and visual findings.
6. Mining Methods — Methods, assumptions, mathematical choices/parameters, and rationale.
7. Results and Evaluation — Patterns discovered and quantitative evaluation.
8. Sensitivity Analysis — How one important decision changed the results.
9. Interpretation and Limitations — What can and cannot be concluded.
10. Ethical / Responsible Use Considerations — Privacy, profiling, bias, misuse, or domain-specific risks.
11. Conclusion — Most important knowledge discovered and recommended next steps.
    - References — At least 3–5 scholarly references for the graduate section.
